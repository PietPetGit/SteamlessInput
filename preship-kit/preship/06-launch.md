# 06 — Launch

**Gate:** the product is being announced publicly. Skip for internal tools and
private projects.

Almost everything here is human-run. The assistant's job is to prepare
artefacts (copy, screenshots, changelog, links, checklists) and to flag what is
missing — not to post anything anywhere. **Never submit, post, or publish to an
external service without explicit approval.**

---

## A. Ready-to-ship gate

Do not launch until:

- Every **P0 and P1** from `01-security.md` is fixed or explicitly accepted in
  writing.
- The app has been run end-to-end on a clean machine/profile from the real
  install or signup path — not just the dev environment.
- Someone who has never used it completed the primary task without help.
- Errors and crashes are being captured somewhere you will actually look.
- There is a rollback plan, and you know how to reach users if something breaks.
- Support contact, pricing (if any), privacy policy, and terms exist and are
  reachable.
- The landing page states in one sentence what it does and for whom.

## B. Launch assets (prepare in-repo)

- One-sentence pitch, a 2–3 sentence description, and a longer post body.
- Screenshots or a short demo clip of the actual product, at a sane resolution.
- Icon/logo in the sizes the destinations ask for.
- A changelog or "what's new" entry.
- The canonical link, with UTM-tagged variants per channel so you can tell what
  worked.

## C. Channels

Pick the handful where the audience actually is. Posting everywhere on day one
is worse than posting well in three places. Read each community's self-promotion
rules first — several of these ban unannounced launch posts.

**Communities**
- Reddit (relevant subreddits; participate before promoting)
- Hacker News (Show HN)
- Indie Hackers
- Quora (answer real questions, do not spam)
- Facebook Groups
- Medium

**Launch platforms & directories**
- Product Hunt
- BetaList
- Uneed
- Launching Next
- StartupBase
- Side Project Ideas
- AlternativeTo
- SaaSHub

**Social**
- X / Twitter
- LinkedIn
- TikTok

## D. After launch

- Watch error rates, latency, spend, and signup funnel for the first 48 hours.
- Answer every comment and support message quickly — launch-day responsiveness
  converts better than the post itself.
- Log what came from where (see the UTM links) and write down which channels
  are worth repeating.
- Feed the feedback back into `03-ux.md` — the first hundred users will find the
  confusing parts you cannot see anymore.
